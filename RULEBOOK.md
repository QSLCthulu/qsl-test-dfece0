## 1. Registration Regulations

### 1.1 Registration and Entry Fee
Teams must register through the official QSL registration process announced by the organizers.  
The entry fee is **0 USD per team**. Payment instructions and the registration link will be published through official QSL channels.  
Registration is complete only after all required details are submitted and payment is confirmed.  
The tournament is limited to **16 accepted teams**. Registration may close once all slots are filled.  
A team captain must be designated during registration. Captain changes must be reported to administration; the replacement captain must be on the approved roster.  
Teams must provide accurate team, player, Steam, and Challengermode profile information. False or misleading information may result in rejection or disqualification.  
Entry fees are non-refundable once a team has been accepted, except where QSL administration announces otherwise or applicable law requires otherwise.

### 1.2 Roster Submission
Teams register five starting players, plus up to two substitutes and one coach.  
Teams must submit each player's display name, Steam account details, and Challengermode profile.  
Players may represent only one team in the tournament. Multi-accounting, account sharing, and playing for multiple teams are prohibited.  
Players must use their own accounts and must not allow another person to play on their behalf.  
Administration may request reasonable evidence to verify a player's identity, account ownership, roster eligibility, or VRS match.  
Roster changes require QSL administration approval where specified. Approval does not waive the fixed-reference-roster requirements in Section 1.3.

### 1.3 Fixed five-player reference roster
The five-player reference roster is fixed for roster-continuity checks:  

- **VRS-matched teams:** the five players on the team's matched official VRS roster in the **Not announced VRS snapshot**.  
- **Non-VRS teams:** the five original starters submitted in the team's accepted registration.  

Teams must field at least **three of the five players** from their applicable reference roster for the entirety of every match, including the Swiss Stage and Playoffs. The qualifying three may differ between matches; teams do not nominate three permanently fixed core players.  
A team is VRS-matched when at least three of its five registered starting players match the same official five-player VRS roster in the applicable snapshot. Substitutes are not counted for initial matching.  
VRS-matched teams must maintain at least three players from the matched VRS roster on their official tournament roster throughout the event. Falling below three may result in disqualification.  
Non-VRS teams must field at least three of their five original registered starters throughout every match.

### 1.4 Substitutions
- **After check-in:** rosters are locked; any change requires QSL administration approval and does not alter the fixed five-player reference roster.  
- **During the Playoffs:** teams may make no more than **Not announced substitute-slot roster changes in total** across the Playoffs, limited to the Sub 1 / Sub 2 slots and subject to administration approval.  
Replacing one player in either substitute slot counts as one change. Replacing both substitute-slot players counts as two changes.  
Using a substitute already listed on the approved roster does not itself count as a substitute-slot roster change.  
Every lineup must continue to meet the applicable three-of-five requirement.  
- **Between matches:** teams may use an approved, registered substitute, provided the lineup meets all eligibility and roster-continuity requirements.  
- **Between maps:** substitutions are allowed only between maps, never during a live map.  
Administration may deny a substitution if it violates roster rules, materially disrupts the match, or undermines competitive integrity.

## 2. Tournament Format and Structure

### 2.1 Tournament Overview
- **Game:** Counter-Strike 2.  
- **Region:** Global.  
- **Competition type:** Online.  
- **Platform:** Challengermode.  
- **Accepted teams:** 16.  
- **Entry fee:** 0 USD per team.  
- **Total advertised prize pool:** 0 USD.  
- **Format:** Tier 2 teams qualify directly for the Playoffs; remaining teams contest a Swiss Stage for the remaining playoff places.  
- **Match format in playoffs:** BO3.  
- **Grand Final format:** BO3, unless the listing says otherwise.  

### 2.2 VRS Direct Playoff Qualification
- Tier 2 teams qualify directly for the Playoffs based on the official VRS ranking snapshot dated **Not announced**.  
- Direct places are awarded to the Tier 2 highest-ranked eligible VRS teams among accepted registered entrants.  
- Direct qualification is a result of the published VRS ranking and the event's open-registration format; it is not an invitation to participate.  
- A team must be registered, accepted, and eligible to receive a direct playoff place.  
- If a team with a potential direct place is not registered or is ruled ineligible, the place passes to the next-highest-ranked eligible registered team.  
- QSL will publish the VRS snapshot date and list of direct qualifiers before the Swiss Stage begins.  
- VRS-matched teams remain subject to the roster-continuity rules in Section 1.3.

### 2.3 Swiss Stage
- The Swiss Stage consists of **Not announced teams**: eligible registered teams that do not receive a direct VRS playoff place.  
- All Swiss Stage matches are **Best-of-One (BO1)**.  
- A team qualifies for the Playoffs after recording **Not announced match wins**.  
- A team is eliminated after recording **Not announced match losses**.  
- Not announced teams qualify from the Swiss Stage to join the Tier 2 VRS direct qualifiers in the Not announced-team Playoff bracket.  
- Teams are paired with opponents on the same or a comparable match record, subject to the pairing method announced by administration.  
- Teams should not face the same opponent more than once in the Swiss Stage unless an unavoidable bracket or administrative issue requires an exception.  
- Swiss pairings, seeding, match windows, and applicable tiebreak procedures will be published before the stage begins.  
- If a team withdraws or is disqualified, QSL determines how its results and remaining pairings are handled, aiming to preserve competitive fairness.  

### 2.4 Playoffs
- The Playoffs consist of **Not announced teams** in a single-elimination bracket: Tier 2 VRS direct qualifiers and Not announced Swiss Stage qualifiers.  
- Standard playoff matches are **BO3**.  
- The Grand Final is **Best-of-Three (BO3)**, unless the event listing states otherwise.  
- A team is eliminated after losing a playoff match.  
- The playoff bracket and seeding method will be published by QSL after Swiss qualification is complete.  
- Rosters lock at check-in. Changes after that need admin approval.

### 2.5 Seeding and Tiebreakers
- Eligible VRS-matched teams are ordered by rank in the fixed **Not announced VRS snapshot**.  
- Remaining teams are seeded below VRS-ranked teams, using documented competitive results and criteria announced by QSL before the draw.  
- Teams without a comparable VRS ranking or competitive record are assessed using the documented information available to administration.  
- Final seeding and the method used will be published before the relevant stage begins.  

## 3. Online Match Rules

### 3.1 Platform Requirements
- All official matches must be played through the designated **Challengermode** tournament infrastructure or another match service explicitly announced by QSL.  
- Every player must have a valid Challengermode account and join the correct match room with the registered account.  
- Match results, match links, brackets, and standings are tracked through the official Challengermode tournament setup and/or channels designated by administration.  
- Players must follow match-room instructions and must not start, abandon, or alter an official match without authorization.  
- A team that cannot access its match room must notify QSL administration immediately and provide evidence of the issue.

### 3.2 Anti-Cheat and Eligibility
- **Not announced Anti-Cheat is mandatory** for every player in official matches.  
- Players must run the anti-cheat required by Challengermode for the full match.  
- A match may not begin if a player fails to meet the Anti-Cheat requirement. QSL administration may issue a forfeit if the issue is not resolved within the allowed start window.  
- Cheating, unauthorized third-party software, account sharing, and attempts to bypass Anti-Cheat are prohibited.  
- Players with active bans that make them ineligible under Challengermode rules or the event's announced eligibility policy may not participate.  
- If a player is banned or found ineligible during the tournament, QSL may investigate previous results and impose penalties, including forfeits or disqualification, where justified.  

### 3.3 Match Settings
- The official Challengermode server configuration takes precedence where it differs from this summary.  
- **Regulation format:** MR12.  
- **Regulation start money:** $800.  
- **Freeze time:** 15 seconds.  
- **Round time:** 1 minute 55 seconds.  
- **Overtime:** at 12–12, overtime is MR3 (best of six rounds) with $10,000 starting money per team.  
- **Overtime continuation:** overtime is repeated until a winner is decided.  
- Custom player models or unauthorized cosmetic modifications that provide an unfair advantage are prohibited.  
- Administration will clarify any server-side setting that differs from this section. Players must not change server settings themselves.

### 3.4 Map Veto
- **BO3 veto:** Team A removes one map → Team B removes one map → Team A picks one map → Team B picks one map → Team A removes one map → Team B removes one map → the remaining map is the decider.  
- Vetoes should be completed immediately before the scheduled match, using the official Challengermode match room or administration instructions.  
- The higher-seeded team is Team A. With no seeding, a coin flip decides.  
- If a team fails to complete its veto on time, QSL administration may make the remaining veto decisions on its behalf.  
- If a BO5 Grand Final is ever announced by the event listing (QSL default is BO3): Team A removes one map → Team B removes one map → Team A picks one map → Team B picks one map → Team A picks one map → Team B picks one map → the remaining map is the final map.  
- Playoffs and finals are best-of-three (BO3) unless the listing says otherwise.  

### 3.5 Servers and Region
- The tournament is an online Global event.  
- Matches use the server selected by Challengermode or designated by QSL for that match.  
- Players are responsible for ensuring their internet connection, hardware, and software are suitable for online competition.  
- A team must report a server-location or routing concern before the match where possible. QSL administration determines whether a server change is appropriate.  

### 3.6 Integrity Verification
- Players must cooperate with reasonable integrity checks requested by QSL administration.  
- QSL may request players to share their screen, provide relevant recordings, or complete other reasonable verification steps where permitted by platform rules and applicable law.  
- Refusal to cooperate with a reasonable and properly communicated integrity check may lead to further investigation or disciplinary action.  
- Any camera requirement will be communicated in advance or explained by QSL when a specific integrity concern arises; it is not automatic unless officially announced.  

## 4. Scheduling

### 4.1 Match Scheduling
- The official schedule is published in `SCHEDULE.md` and through QSL's official channels.  
- All displayed times use the time zone specified in the official schedule. If no time zone is stated, teams must ask QSL rather than assume.  
- Teams are responsible for checking match windows, confirming match arrangements, and being available during the announced period.  
- Captains must coordinate through the official match chat or designated QSL communication channel.  
- A proposed time is not approved until the opponent and, where required, QSL administration has confirmed it.  

### 4.2 Rescheduling
- Reschedule requests must be made in the official match chat or support channel and include the match link, reason, and proposed alternative times.  
- During the Swiss Stage, a reschedule is valid only if it stays within the published round window and complies with the official schedule.  
- Playoff reschedules require QSL administration approval.  
- QSL may deny a reschedule that delays the tournament, creates an unfair competitive advantage, or conflicts with later matches.  
- Teams must not use rescheduling to avoid an opponent, gain unfair preparation time, or delay a result.  
- If teams cannot agree on a time, they must contact QSL before the deadline. QSL may set a mandatory match time.  

### 4.3 Punctuality and Forfeits
- Teams must be ready at the confirmed match start time, with eligible players available and Not announced functioning.  
- Teams must be in the server and ready within 10 minutes of the scheduled start, or may forfeit the first map.  
- A match not completed within its round window without an approved reschedule may be recorded as a forfeit.  
- Administration may consider documented platform outages or exceptional circumstances before confirming a forfeit.  
- Teams must report disputes during the match by pausing and contacting an admin, with evidence where possible.  

## 5. Technical Issues

### 5.1 Pauses and Timeouts
- Technical pauses are for real problems only (crash, disconnect, hardware). Announce the reason in chat immediately.  
- Each team gets four tactical timeouts of 30 seconds per map, taken during freeze time.  
- Total technical pause time is capped at 10 minutes per team per map unless an admin extends it.  
- Teams must not use technical pauses to gain an unfair tactical advantage, disrupt an opponent, or delay a match without cause.  
- QSL administration may shorten, extend, or deny a pause based on the circumstances and tournament schedule.  

### 5.2 Disconnections
- If a player disconnects, the affected team must use the in-game pause feature where available and notify the opponent and QSL administration as soon as possible.  
- Teams must not resume play until the issue has been addressed according to server rules and QSL instructions.  
- The response depends on match state, server capability, platform rules, and whether the issue affects one or both teams.  
- If a player disconnects before the first kill of round one, the round is restarted.  
- After that, play continues; the team should take a technical pause and wait for the player to reconnect.  
- If the player cannot return within the pause limit, the team may bring in a registered substitute or continue 4v5.  
- Repeated or unexplained disconnections may result in a forfeit if a team cannot complete the match.  

### 5.3 Game Updates
- If a CS2 update or platform maintenance prevents the match from running properly, teams must pause where possible and contact QSL.  
- Teams must not independently restart or reschedule an affected official match without approval.  
- QSL decides whether to delay, reschedule, restart, or continue the match based on platform status and competitive fairness.  

### 5.4 Internet and Hardware Issues
- Players are responsible for their internet connection, PC, peripherals, drivers, and software.  
- A personal power, hardware, or ISP issue does not automatically require a replay or reschedule.  
- Teams must report a major connection or platform-wide issue immediately and provide evidence where possible.  
- QSL may grant an exception when a documented, widespread technical incident materially affects the match and a remedy is necessary to protect competitive integrity.  

## 6. Broadcast and Media

### 6.1 Player Streaming
- Players may stream their own point of view unless QSL administration announces a restriction for a specific match.  
- Player POV streams must use at least a **Not announced delay** if required by QSL administration to protect match integrity.  
- Stream titles should identify QSL where practical and must not falsely imply that a personal stream is an official QSL broadcast.  
- Streamers must retain a recording or VOD if requested by QSL for a protest or integrity review.  
- Streaming permission does not allow players or coaches to watch a live broadcast of their own ongoing match or receive outside information about it.  
- QSL may require a stream to be delayed, modified, or stopped if it creates a competitive integrity or rights issue.  

### 6.2 Community Broadcasting
- Community casters must request permission through the official QSL contact channel before broadcasting.  
- Approved broadcasters must follow technical, delay, branding, and conduct conditions provided by QSL.  
- Participation in the tournament or access to a match room does not automatically grant broadcast permission.  
- QSL may approve, restrict, or revoke community broadcast permission to protect competitive integrity or official broadcast arrangements.  

### 6.3 Spectator Access
- Spectator or GOTV/CSTV access may be granted only through the approved method and at QSL's discretion.  
- Passwords, server addresses, and restricted spectator details must not be shared with unauthorized persons.  
- Spectator access does not authorize rebroadcasting the match or sharing live competitive information with a team.  
- QSL may revoke spectator access if it is misused.  

### 6.4 Official Broadcast Rights
- QSL may broadcast selected official matches itself or through approved partners.  
- Official broadcast delays and production conditions will be set by QSL and communicated to broadcasters.  
- Re-streaming or retransmitting an official QSL or partner broadcast requires prior written permission.  
- Permission to stream a player's own POV or community-cast a match does not automatically permit use of the official broadcast feed.  
- Broadcast violations may result in a stream being stopped, access being revoked, or further penalties where appropriate.  

### 6.5 Co-Streaming
- Co-streaming an official QSL broadcast requires prior written approval.  
- Approved co-streamers must follow all conditions provided by QSL, including branding, delay, and content restrictions.  
- Co-stream approval does not automatically grant independent spectator access or permission to produce a separate community broadcast.  
- QSL may deny or revoke co-stream permission to protect rights holders, competitive integrity, or event operations.  

## 7. Penalties and Prohibited Conduct

### 7.1 Cheating and Third-Party Software
- Cheating of any kind is prohibited and results in disqualification and a ban from QSL events.  
- Unauthorized third-party software, scripts, hardware, or modifications that provide an unfair advantage are prohibited.  
- Account sharing, impersonation, and allowing an unregistered player to compete are prohibited.  
- Attempting to bypass Not announced or an integrity check is a serious violation.  
- Custom models or unauthorized files that provide a competitive advantage are prohibited.  
- If a player is found to have cheated or becomes ineligible during the event, QSL may review the team's results and impose forfeits or disqualification where justified.  

### 7.2 Exploits and Illegal Actions
- Deliberately exploiting a game bug for an unfair advantage is prohibited.  
- Pixel walking, use of invisible or unintended ledges, and other prohibited map exploits are not allowed where they violate CS2 competitive rules or event instructions.  
- Players must not use unintended bomb-plant locations or glitches to make the objective inaccessible or unfair.  
- Players must not intentionally join the opposing team or manipulate team assignment to gain an advantage.  
- Boosting or clipping through solid geometry to gain an illegal position is prohibited.  
- Players must report a serious unintended exploit to QSL administration rather than continue abusing it.  
- Penalties depend on the nature and impact of the violation and may include a round loss, map loss, match forfeit, or disqualification.  

### 7.3 Communication and Behaviour
- Players, coaches, and team representatives must treat opponents, admins, casters, and other participants respectfully.  
- Racist, discriminatory, threatening, or harassing conduct is prohibited.  
- Serious discriminatory abuse may result in a match forfeit, removal from the event, or a tournament ban. Repeated offences may lead to stronger penalties.  
- Threats, doxxing, targeted harassment, and attempts to intimidate an opponent or administrator are serious violations.  
- Publicly sharing private administrative discussions, confidential investigation material, or private personal information without authorization may result in penalties.  
- All participants must follow reasonable instructions from tournament administration and uphold fair play.  

### 7.4 Stream Sniping and Outside Information
- Ghosting (watching the stream for information) results in disqualification and a ban from QSL events.  
- This restriction applies between maps and during technical pauses.  
- Teams must not receive live information from spectators, casters, friends, or other outside sources.  
- Suspected stream sniping or outside communication will be investigated using available evidence.  
- Proven violations may result in a round or match loss, disqualification, or a ban.  

### 7.5 Betting and Match Manipulation
- Players, coaches and staff may not bet on any match in a tournament they take part in, or share private information with anyone who does.  
- Match-fixing, intentionally losing, collusion, result manipulation, or attempts to influence another team to manipulate a match are prohibited.  
- Participants must report credible approaches involving match manipulation to QSL administration.  
- Violations may result in immediate disqualification, prize forfeiture, and referral to the relevant platform or competition integrity body where appropriate.  

### 7.6 Repeated Violations
- Repeated violations may result in escalating penalties.  
- QSL administration may remove a team whose conduct repeatedly disrupts the event or undermines competitive integrity.  
- A team is responsible for the conduct of its players, coach, and representatives participating in event activities.  
- Penalties may include a warning, round loss, map loss, match forfeit, disqualification, prize forfeiture, or exclusion from future QSL events.  

## 8. Tournament Administration

### 8.1 Administrative Authority
- QSL administration interprets and enforces this Rulebook.  
- Administration may investigate incidents, request evidence, correct results, and issue penalties to protect fairness and operation of the tournament.  
- Situations not covered here are decided by admins in the spirit of fair play.  
- Admin decisions are final.  

### 8.2 Official Communication
- Teams must use the official QSL communication channel announced by the organizers for support requests, roster requests, and protests.  
- Captains are responsible for communicating administrative decisions and match information to their teams.  
- Teams should keep match links, screenshots, recordings, and relevant correspondence until the event and any related protest period have concluded.  
- A request sent through an unofficial channel may not be treated as a formal report or protest.  
- QSL's official social media is Not announced. Social media posts do not replace formal protest submission through the designated support channel.  

### 8.3 Protests and Appeals
- Formal protests must be submitted through the official QSL support channel.  
- A protest must identify the match, the rule allegedly violated, the teams and players involved, and the remedy requested.  
- Supporting evidence should include match links, screenshots, demo or recording timestamps, and relevant messages where available.  
- Protests should be filed within **Not announced after the match concludes**, unless the schedule or administration announces a different deadline for a specific stage. Administration may decline late protests unless exceptional circumstances justify review.  
- Teams must not delay a protest until it becomes strategically advantageous.  
- Administration may request further evidence from either team or from the platform.  
- Where practicable, an appeal is reviewed by a senior administrator not directly responsible for the original decision.  
- Final decisions and resulting standings changes will be communicated to affected teams. Confidential personal or investigation details may remain private.

### 10. Frequently Asked Questions
- **Q: Do all players need a Challengermode account and Not announced?**  
  A: Yes. Every player must use their own eligible Challengermode account and have Not announced running for official matches.  
- **Q: How does VRS qualification work?**  
  A: Tier 2 eligible, registered teams with the highest rankings in the fixed **Not announced VRS snapshot** receive direct places in the Playoffs. These are direct playoff qualifications within an open-registration tournament, not invitations to participate.  
- **Q: What happens to teams that do not receive a direct VRS playoff place?**  
  A: They compete in the Swiss Stage. Swiss Stage matches are best-of-one (BO1). A team qualifies after Not announced wins and is eliminated after Not announced losses. Not announced teams advance to the Playoffs.  
- **Q: What counts as a team's core?**  
  A: For a VRS-matched team, the reference roster is the five players on its matched VRS roster in the **Not announced snapshot**. For a non-VRS team, it is the five original registered starters. At least three players from the applicable reference roster must play the entirety of every match.  
- **Q: Can we change our roster?**  
  A: Rosters lock at check-in; changes after that require QSL administration approval and do not change the fixed reference roster. During the Playoffs, up to Not announced changes to the Sub 1 / Sub 2 slots are permitted, subject to approval.  
- **Q: Does using an already registered substitute count as a roster change?**  
  A: Simply fielding a substitute already listed on the approved roster does not count as a substitute-slot roster change. Every lineup must still satisfy the applicable three-of-five requirement.  
- **Q: Can a player be substituted during a match?**  
  A: Substitutions are allowed only between maps, never during a live map.  
- **Q: What if a player disconnects?**  
  A: Pause where the platform allows, notify the opponent and QSL administration immediately, and follow their instructions. A replay is not automatic.  
- **Q: Can I stream my POV or cast a match?**  
  A: Player POV streaming may be allowed subject to integrity conditions. Community broadcasting and co-streaming require prior approval from QSL.  
- **Q: How do I submit a protest?**  
  A: Use the official QSL support channel and submit evidence. The standard deadline is Not announced after the match concludes unless a different deadline is announced.  
- **Q: Who makes the final decision on an issue not covered here?**  
  A: QSL tournament administration, applying the principles of competitive integrity and fairness.
